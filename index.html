CHAPTER I — ABOUT

Improving care for severe mental illness, locally and globally.

Dr. Rick Wolthusen is a psychiatrist in the Division of Psychotic Disorders at McLean Hospital and an Instructor in Psychiatry at Harvard Medical School. His clinical work focuses on people living with psychosis, bipolar disorder, and catatonia — including complex, recurrent forms of catatonia that may require longer-term management.

His research grows directly out of clinical practice. He has published on catatonia across a range of settings, including low-resource environments and different linguistic and cultural contexts, and serves on the Medical Advisory Board of the Catatonia Foundation. He is also Head of Training and Education for the Chester M. Pierce Division of Global Psychiatry at Massachusetts General Hospital.

A central theme of his work is strengthening the connection between specialist psychiatric care and the communities in which patients live — partnering with patients, families, community health workers, and religious and community leaders to improve recognition of severe mental illness, reduce stigma, and support sustained engagement with care. This work spans Ghana, Kenya, Rwanda, Ecuador, South Africa, Australia, and New Zealand.

A growing part of his work is bringing lessons from global mental health back to the United States: adapting community- and culturally informed approaches to inpatient psychiatry, and training clinicians, chaplains, and other members of care teams to better recognise severe mental illness, suicide risk, and the cultural contexts that shape psychiatric care.
